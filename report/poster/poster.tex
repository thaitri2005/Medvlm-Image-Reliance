\begin{columns}
\column{0.5}

\block{Background: is the model looking at the image?}{
Medical VQA benchmarks score whether an answer is right, not whether the model looked at the image. A model can
answer ``yes, there is a nodule'' from language priors alone, and then fail exactly on the patients whose images
are unusual. Paraphrase consistency does not help: a model that ignores the image is often \emph{more}
consistent, because nothing visual perturbs it.

\vspace{4mm}
The \emph{consistent-but-dangerous} taxonomy (Sadanandan \& Behzadan, CVPR-W 2026) therefore crosses two
per-answer tests: is the answer stable under paraphrase, and does it change when the image is removed?

\vspace{5mm}
\begin{center}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
 & \textbf{changes} without the image & \textbf{same} without the image\\
\midrule
\textbf{stable} under paraphrase & \textsc{Ideal} & \dng\\
\textbf{flips} under paraphrase & \textsc{Fragile} & \textsc{Worst}\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{3mm}
A \dng{} answer looks trustworthy but does not depend on the image.

\vspace{5mm}
\innerblock{}{A taxonomy like this invites comparisons between models. We ask whether its reliance test,
\textbf{removing the image}, ranks models the same way as probes that \textbf{keep an image in the input}.}
}

\column{0.5}

\block{Method}{
\textbf{Three model pairs}, each a medical model and the general model it was trained from (same architecture
and size): \med{MedGemma-4B} and \med{MedGemma-1.5-4B} vs.\ \gen{Gemma-3-4B}; \med{MedVLThinker-3B} vs.\
its base \gen{Qwen2.5-VL-3B}.

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Data.} 3,365 questions on 907 images from SLAKE (CT, MRI, X-ray), VQA-RAD (radiology) and PathVQA
(pathology); 1,206 yes/no test questions are the primary set.

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Probes.} Each changes only the image or the instruction. A model that uses the image should change its
answer when the image changes, so for each probe we measure the share of yes/no answers that change.

\vspace{3mm}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.66\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Image removed} & image tokens omitted: the taxonomy's own test\\
Wrong image & a random image, same dataset and modality\\
Black image & a black image: format kept, content removed\\
\textbf{Opposite-answer image} & an image where the \emph{same question} has the opposite answer: the most direct
test\\
``Unknown'' allowed & image removed; the model may decline\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Scoring and setup.} P(yes) from the yes/no token logits in one forward pass; the total yes+no probability
shows when a model would rather say something else (``please provide the image''). Five models, identical
prompts, bf16, greedy; about 120,000 calls on one 16\,GB GPU; 95\% CIs from a bootstrap over images.

\vspace{4mm}
\innerblock{}{\textbf{Pre-registered.} Endpoints frozen before the main run; every later probe and model pair
registered (a dated git tag) before it ran. Failed predictions are reported.}
}

\end{columns}

% ================================================================== row 3: why + take-aways | finding 2 + corrections + limits
\begin{columns}
\column{0.5}

\block{Why: what image removal picks up instead}{
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.55\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Refusal.} Asked yes or no about an image it was not given, \gen{Gemma-3} would rather ask for it on
\textbf{29\%} of questions, \med{MedGemma-4B} on 18\%, \med{MedGemma-1.5} on 0.7\%, the Qwen models never. Forced
answers there are unrelated to the answers with the image ($\kappa \approx 0$), so half count as
``image-reliant'': refusing more looks like relying on the image more.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.42\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../figures/fig4_abstention.pdf}
\end{minipage}
\figcap{Right: questions declined without the image. Forced yes/no (top): Gemma-3 declines far more. ``Unknown''
allowed (bottom): both decline on $\ge$93\%.}

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Priors that match the image.} If a blind guess matches what the image shows, the answer does not change
and image removal calls it blind. This explains the Qwen pair, where nobody refuses, and MedGemma-4B's extra
\dng{} answers, which are \textbf{correct}: +14.1 points \dng-and-correct, \mn0.4 \dng-and-wrong.
}

\block{Take-aways for reliance audits}{
\begin{enumerate}
\item Don't compare models by a \dng{} share from image removal: it tracks refusal and priors.
\item Probe in format, ideally with an image whose correct answer is the opposite.
\item Run that probe on data outside each model's training set.
\item Report the chance floor, split by correctness, let models abstain.
\end{enumerate}
}

\column{0.5}

\block{Finding 2: the in-format advantage stays in the training data}{
Both MedGemmas were trained on SLAKE, where the in-format probes favoured them. We registered a test on
\textbf{PathVQA}, which no medical model was trained on, before running it:

\vspace{3mm}
\begin{center}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Follows the opposite-answer image & \textbf{SLAKE} & \textbf{PathVQA}\\
medical \mn{} general, points & \muted{trained on} & \muted{held out}\\
\midrule
\med{MedGemma-4B} \mn{} \gen{Gemma-3} & +48.0 & +0.8 \muted{[\mn2.7, 4.2]}\\
\med{MedGemma-1.5} \mn{} \gen{Gemma-3} & +36.5 & \mn5.6 \muted{[\mn9.1, \mn2.0]}\\
\med{MedVLThinker} \mn{} \gen{Qwen2.5-VL} & \mn1.6 & \mn0.9 \muted{[\mn3.0, 1.3]}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{3mm}
Both MedGemma predictions failed, so under the rule fixed in advance the claim that they use the image more was
\textbf{withdrawn}: the SLAKE gap reflects training data. All five models do read pathology images, to a similar
degree (right on both images of a swap 36--43\% of the time; a model ignoring the image: 0\%).
}

\block{The \dng{} share needs correcting}{
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Chance floor:} 36--42\% of yes/no answers look image-independent even if unrelated to the image.
\item \textbf{Correctness:} the extra \dng{} answers are mostly correct, from good priors.
\item \textbf{No warning signal:} image reliance flags wrong answers at chance (AUROC \textbf{0.51}; confidence
0.71).
\end{itemize}
}

\block{Limits}{
\small Three pairs from two families: the problem exists, how common it is we cannot say. The explanations were
formed after the data. The held-out test is pathology only. Paraphrases were LLM-judged (a 50-item check by the
author, with AI assistance: 88\%). Not a clinical claim.\\[2mm]
\muted{\footnotesize Sadanandan \& Behzadan, CVPR-W 2026; Zafar et al.\ 2026; Lotfinia et al.\ 2026; Sellergren
et al.\ (MedGemma) 2025.}
}
