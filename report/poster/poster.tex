\begin{columns}
\column{0.5}

\block{Background}{
Medical VQA benchmarks measure whether an answer is correct, not whether the model used the image. An answer
derived from language priors can be correct on typical cases and fail precisely on atypical images. Consistency
under rephrasing does not resolve this: a model that ignores the image can be \emph{more} consistent, since no
visual signal perturbs it.

\vspace{4mm}
The \emph{consistent-but-dangerous} taxonomy (Sadanandan \& Behzadan, CVPR-W 2026) therefore combines two
per-answer criteria: stability under paraphrase, and whether the answer changes when the image is removed.

\vspace{5mm}
\begin{center}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
 & \textbf{changes} without the image & \textbf{unchanged} without the image\\
\midrule
\textbf{stable} under paraphrase & \textsc{Ideal} & \dng\\
\textbf{unstable} under paraphrase & \textsc{Fragile} & \textsc{Worst}\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{3mm}
A \dng{} answer appears reliable but does not depend on the image.

\vspace{5mm}
\innerblock{}{\textbf{Question.} Does the taxonomy's reliance criterion, \textbf{image removal}, rank models in the
same order as \textbf{in-format probes}, which keep an image in the input?}
}

\column{0.5}

\block{Method}{
\textbf{Models.} Three pairs, each a medical model and the general-purpose model it was derived from (same
architecture and size): \med{MedGemma-4B} and \med{MedGemma-1.5-4B} vs.\ \gen{Gemma-3-4B}; \med{MedVLThinker-3B}
vs.\ \gen{Qwen2.5-VL-3B}.

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Data.} 3,365 questions on 907 images from SLAKE (CT, MRI, X-ray), VQA-RAD (radiology) and PathVQA
(pathology); the primary set comprises 1,206 yes/no test questions.

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Probes.} Each probe alters only the image or the instruction. A model that uses the image should change its
answer when the image changes; for each probe we report the \emph{answer-change rate}.

\vspace{3mm}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.66\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Image removed} & image tokens omitted; the taxonomy's criterion\\
Different image & a random image of the same dataset and modality\\
Black image & format preserved, content removed\\
\textbf{Opposite-answer image} & an image for which the \emph{same question} has the opposite answer; the most
direct test\\
Abstention permitted & image removed; the model may answer ``unknown''\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Scoring.} P(yes) from the yes/no token logits in a single forward pass; the total yes/no probability
identifies refusals (``please provide the image''). Identical prompts, bf16, greedy decoding; about 120,000 model
calls on one 16\,GB GPU; 95\% CIs from a bootstrap over images.

\vspace{4mm}
\innerblock{}{\textbf{Pre-registration.} Endpoints were fixed before the main run, and each subsequent probe and
model pair was registered (as a dated git tag) before it was run. Unsupported predictions are reported.}
}

\end{columns}

\begin{columns}
\column{0.5}

\block{Sources of the image-removal differences}{
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.55\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Refusal.} Without the image, \gen{Gemma-3} declines on \textbf{29\%} of questions, \med{MedGemma-4B} on 18\%,
\med{MedGemma-1.5} on 0.7\%, and the Qwen-based models on none. Forced answers on declined questions are unrelated
to the answers given with the image ($\kappa \approx 0$), so about half are classified as image-reliant: a higher
refusal rate inflates apparent reliance.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.42\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../figures/fig4_abstention.pdf}
\end{minipage}
\figcap{Right: questions declined without the image. Forced yes/no format (top): Gemma-3 declines far more often.
Abstention permitted (bottom): both models decline on $\ge$93\%.}

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Agreement between prior and image.} When a model's prior matches the image content, its answer does not
change without the image, and image removal classifies it as image-independent. This accounts for the differences
in the Qwen pair, in which neither model declines, and for MedGemma-4B's additional \dng{} answers, which are
\textbf{correct}: +14.1 points \dng-and-correct, \mn0.4 \dng-and-incorrect.
}

\block{Recommendations for reliance audits}{
\begin{enumerate}
\item Do not compare models by a \dng{} share obtained by image removal; it reflects refusal and priors.
\item Assess reliance with in-format probes, preferably an image that requires the opposite answer.
\item Evaluate those probes on data outside each model's training set.
\item Report the chance floor, decompose by correctness, and permit abstention.
\end{enumerate}
}

\column{0.5}

\block{Finding 2: the in-format advantage is confined to training data}{
Both MedGemma models were trained on SLAKE, where the in-format probes favoured them. We registered a test on
\textbf{PathVQA}, which none of the medical models was trained on, before running it:

\vspace{3mm}
\begin{center}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Follows the opposite-answer image & \textbf{SLAKE} & \textbf{PathVQA}\\
medical \mn{} general, points & \muted{training data} & \muted{held out}\\
\midrule
\med{MedGemma-4B} \mn{} \gen{Gemma-3} & +48.0 & +0.8 \muted{[\mn2.7, 4.2]}\\
\med{MedGemma-1.5} \mn{} \gen{Gemma-3} & +36.5 & \mn5.6 \muted{[\mn9.1, \mn2.0]}\\
\med{MedVLThinker} \mn{} \gen{Qwen2.5-VL} & \mn1.6 & \mn0.9 \muted{[\mn3.0, 1.3]}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{3mm}
Both MedGemma predictions were unsupported, so under the decision rule fixed in advance the hypothesis that they
rely on the image more was \textbf{withdrawn}: the SLAKE difference reflects training exposure.
}

\block{Corrections to the \dng{} share}{
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Chance floor:} 36--42\% of yes/no answers appear image-independent by chance alone.
\item \textbf{Correctness:} the additional \dng{} answers are predominantly correct.
\item \textbf{Error detection:} image reliance identifies errors at chance level (AUROC \textbf{0.51}; model
confidence 0.71).
\end{itemize}
}

\block{Limitations}{
\small Three model pairs establish that the disagreement occurs, not how often. The mechanistic accounts are post hoc;
the held-out test covers pathology only. Paraphrase
equivalence was judged by an LLM (a 50-item check by the first author, with AI assistance, accepted 88\%). No clinical
claims are made.\\[2mm]
\muted{\footnotesize Sadanandan \& Behzadan, CVPR-W 2026; Zafar et al.\ 2026; Lotfinia et al.\ 2026; Sellergren
et al.\ (MedGemma) 2025.}
}
